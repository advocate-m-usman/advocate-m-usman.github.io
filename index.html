    Preparing for a consultation

    Bring the documents that tell your story

    A clear record helps identify the work needed. Where available, bring copies of the relevant deeds or agreements, payment records, correspondence and any court notices or orders.

    For a drafting request, explain who the parties are, what has been agreed and the purpose of the document. Do not sign incomplete papers or leave important terms undecided.

    Drafting, execution, stamping, registration and attestation are distinct steps. The formalities required will be considered for the particular document.

    PropertyKey checks before signing a sale deed+

    Start with the seller’s authority, the property record and the terms of payment—not just the wording on the final page.

    Identify the property and the seller

    Check that the property description is consistent across the proposed deed, earlier transfer documents and available land or society records. Ask who owns the property, who is in possession and whether the person signing has authority to do so. Where an attorney, company or estate is involved, the authority documents need attention as well.

    Review the record, not just one paper

    Gather the chain of transfer documents, available ownership records, payment receipts and any relevant approvals or correspondence. Ask about existing claims, mortgages, tenancy arrangements and pending proceedings. Resolve differences in names, area, boundaries or property identifiers before completing the transaction. The Punjab Land Records Authority provides public land-record services; the records required depend on the property concerned.

    Agree the commercial terms clearly

    The written terms should identify the price, payment stages, handover arrangements, responsibility for dues and the documents to be supplied. Discuss what happens if a party cannot complete the transaction. Keep a traceable record of payments and avoid signing papers with blank spaces.

    Confirm the transfer formalities

    An agreement to sell and a completed transfer should not be treated as interchangeable. Ask which execution, stamping and registration steps apply before relying on a document as proof of ownership. See the Transfer of Property Act, 1882 as a statutory starting point, alongside the law and records applicable to the transaction.

    For your consultation

    Bring the proposed deed or agreement, earlier title papers, available land or society records, payment evidence and any notice about the property.

    Criminal LawUnderstanding bail in Pakistan+

    The FIR, the alleged offences and the stage of proceedings are the starting points for a useful discussion about bail.

    Begin with the case record

    Bring the FIR number, police station, alleged offence provisions and copies of any available orders. Explain whether an arrest has occurred, whether an application has already been filed and whether a hearing is scheduled. An account of the events is useful, but it should be checked against the actual record.

    Different situations require different applications

    The Code of Criminal Procedure distinguishes bail in bailable and non-bailable matters and gives the High Court and Court of Session powers concerning bail. Sections 496–498 are relevant starting points. Pre-arrest and post-arrest situations require separate assessment, and special laws may affect the applicable procedure. Consult the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1898 and the current provisions applicable to the case.

    Prepare accurate supporting information

    Organise the relevant identity documents, earlier applications, medical or other supporting records and court orders. Tell your advocate about previous proceedings concerning the same matter. Do not alter records, contact witnesses improperly or assume that filing an application itself prevents arrest.

    Read and follow the order

    If relief is granted, the wording of the order matters. Confirm any requirements relating to appearance, bonds, sureties or participation in the proceedings. Bail does not itself decide the final outcome of the criminal case.

    For urgent matters

    Call directly and provide the FIR details and any known hearing or arrest information. A website message is not a court filing or confirmation that representation has been accepted.

    Family LawKhula, maintenance and custody: preparing for advice+

    Separate the questions about the marriage, financial arrangements and children so that each can be considered properly.

    Identify the assistance you need

    A family consultation may involve dissolution of marriage, dower or haq mehr, maintenance, recovery of belongings, or arrangements for children. Explain your immediate concern and any existing proceedings. The appropriate route depends on the facts, applicable personal law and the forum; one person’s case should not be treated as a prediction for another.

    Bring the marriage and financial records

    Where available, bring the nikahnama, identity documents, relevant notices, earlier orders and records relating to dower or other financial arrangements. For a maintenance discussion, organise information about regular expenses, payments already made and the financial material you can lawfully provide. Keep original documents secure.

    Keep children’s arrangements specific

    Prepare accurate information about the children’s ages, schooling, health needs and current living arrangements. Explain any existing custody or visitation order and what practical arrangement you are seeking. Avoid drawing children into the dispute or using public posts to discuss sensitive family details.

    Ask about the scope of the proceedings

    Discuss which requests need to be made, what evidence is available and whether any immediate application is appropriate. The Family Courts Act, 1964, as published by the Punjab Code, is one reference for family-court matters in Punjab. Current amendments, personal law and relevant orders must also be considered.

    Protect sensitive information

    Start with a brief description of the issue. Agree how to share personal records before sending identity documents, information about children or intimate family details.

    Intellectual PropertyPreparing for a trademark application+

    Identify the owner, the mark and the goods or services before preparing the application documents.

    Identify the applicant

    Confirm who will own the mark: an individual, a company or another legal entity. Make sure the applicant’s name and supporting records are consistent. Where someone is acting on the applicant’s behalf, discuss the authorisation documents required.

    Define the mark and its use

    Bring a clear version of the name or logo, a description of the goods or services and any evidence of existing use. Explain whether you seek protection for a word, a logo or a combination. A logo designer’s files, a business registration and a trademark application serve different purposes; make sure the intended work is clear.

    Ask about searches and filing requirements

    Before filing, discuss relevant searches, the appropriate goods or services classification, the application documents and official fees. A search can inform the advice but should not be treated as a promise that a mark will be accepted.

    Plan for follow-up

    Keep application references, receipts and correspondence. Any objection or other communication needs review on its own terms, including the applicable response deadline. Submitting an application is not a guarantee of registration.

    Official information

    IPO here means the Intellectual Property Organization of Pakistan, not a stock-market initial public offering. Contact the practice to discuss trademark filing and related IP documentation.
